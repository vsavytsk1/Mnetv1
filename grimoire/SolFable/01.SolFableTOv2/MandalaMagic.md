# What Came Before the Titans: Greek Pre-Titanic Cosmogony, Vedic Cosmogony, and Constructible Geometry

## TL;DR
- **In the Greek tradition there is no single "before the Titans" — there are several competing cosmogonies.** Hesiod's stratum (Chaos → Gaia/Tartaros/Eros → Ouranos → Titans) is the best-attested; the Orphic egg/Phanes material and Pherecydes' geometric-textile cosmos are genuinely archaic in kernel but survive largely through late Neoplatonic reconstruction that must not be read back as "original." The Vedic parallels (Rigveda 10.129, 10.121, 10.90) are real but late within the Rigveda (Book 10), and the shared "cosmic egg" is **OPEN/CONTESTED**, not a proven common inheritance.
- **The securely established Indo-European link is linguistic, not cosmogonic:** \*Dyēus ph₂tēr → Zeus / Dyaus Pitṛ is EXACT; Lincoln's Manu/Yemo first-sacrifice is a broadly accepted CONDITIONAL reconstruction; Dumézil's trifunctionalism and 19th-c. solar mythology (Müller) are substantially discredited and should not be leaned on.
- **The geometry is buildable and precisely specified on both sides:** the Śulba-sūtra falcon altar (five layers, 200 bricks/layer, area 7½ square puruṣas, √2 ≈ 577/408) and Plato's five regular solids with the world-soul number series (1,2,3,4,8,9,27) are EXACT/provable; Anaximander's 9/18/27 wheels are a Diels–Tannery CONDITIONAL reconstruction; the Kalachakra mandala grid (11th c. CE) is EXACT to its manuals but must not be conflated with Vedic material.

## Key Findings

1. **"Before the Titans" is not one question but at least five separate ancient answers** (Hesiodic, Orphic, Pherecydean, Homeric-Okeanos, and the Alcman/Epimenides/Musaeus minor cosmogonies). Eudemus (via later doxography) already catalogued this diversity in the 4th c. BCE.
2. **The philology is unambiguous on one point the user flagged:** Greek *χάος* (kháos) means "gap/chasm/gaping void," from the root meaning "to gape/yawn" (χάσκω/χαίνω, cognate with English *yawn*) — NOT "disorder." The "chaos = disorder" sense is a later (Ovidian/Latin) development.
3. **The Orphic cosmic egg and Phanes are attested early in kernel but elaborated late.** The Derveni papyrus — officially inscribed on UNESCO's Memory of the World Register (6 October 2015) as "the oldest surviving book in Europe," the physical roll dated c. 340–320 BCE (reign of Philip II of Macedon), copying a poem composed c. 420–410 BCE — is the oldest witness. Strikingly, in it **Phanes may not appear at all** and the egg is not clearly present; the full egg→Phanes→swallowing-by-Zeus system is known mainly from the **Rhapsodic** and **Hieronyman** theogonies preserved by **Damascius, Proclus, and Athenagoras** (2nd–6th c. CE). This gap of 700–900 years is the single most important caveat in the Greek half.
4. **The Vedic "Hymn of Creation" (RV 10.129) ends in genuine agnosticism** — "The gods came afterwards, with the creation of this universe. Who then knows whence it has arisen?" (10.129.6); and 10.129.7: whether it formed itself or not, "the one who looks down on it from the highest heaven, only he knows — or perhaps he does not know." This is EXACT to the text.
5. **The strongest comparative result is linguistic**: \*Dyēus ph₂tēr is a secure reconstruction with reflexes in Zeus patēr, Dyáuṣ Pitṛ́, Jupiter, and Palaic Tiyaz papaz. Beyond such cognate pairs, cosmogonic parallels weaken rapidly.
6. **The Vedic altar geometry is fully constructible**: the Baudhāyana Śulba-sūtra states the "Pythagorean" diagonal rule, lists five primitive Pythagorean triples, gives an area-preserving square↔circle procedure, and a √2 approximation accurate to five decimals.
7. **Plato's Timaeus gives a complete, buildable cosmological geometry**: four elements ↔ four regular solids from two triangle types, the dodecahedron reserved "for the whole," and a world-soul built on the double/triple geometric series 1,2,3,4,8,9,27 filled with harmonic and arithmetic means and 256/243 leftover intervals.
8. **The Kalachakra mandala is precisely specified but chronologically late** (Kālacakra Tantra and Vimalaprabhā introduced to Tibet 1027 CE); its 624-*mus* grid diameter and nested square palaces are EXACT to Tibetan iconometric manuals but are Buddhist Vajrayāna, ~1,800 years younger than the Vedic material — putting a Kalachakra mandala on a "Vedas" cover is a documented category error.

## Details — A Source-by-Source Ledger

### PART 1 — GREEK COSMOGONY BEFORE THE TITANS

**1.1 Hesiod, *Theogony* (c. 700 BCE).** [EXACT — extant primary text]
Order of emergence (lines 116–122): first **Chaos** (Χάος), then **Gaia** (Γαῖα, broad-breasted Earth), **Tartaros** (Τάρταρα, in the depths of the wide-pathed earth), and **Eros** (Ἔρος, "fairest among immortals"). From Chaos are *born* (unambiguously) **Erebos** (Ἔρεβος, gloom/darkness) and **Nyx** (Νύξ, Night); from Nyx and Erebos come **Aither** (Αἰθήρ, bright upper air) and **Hemera** (Ἡμέρα, Day). Gaia parthenogenetically produces **Ouranos** (Οὐρανός, Starry Sky), the Mountains, and **Pontos** (Πόντος, Sea). Gaia + Ouranos then generate the twelve **Titans** (Okeanos, Koios, Krios, Hyperion, Iapetos, Theia, Rhea, Themis, Mnemosyne, Phoibe, Tethys, and Kronos). So in Hesiod the Titans are the **third** cosmogonic generation; four "primordial" beings (the Protogenoi) precede them.

*Philological note on Chaos* [EXACT]: χάος derives from a PIE root meaning "gape/yawn" (cf. χάσκω/χαίνω "to gape," English *yawn*), so it denotes a **chasm/gap/void**, the space that opens (later) between earth and sky — **not** "disorder." The "turbulent mixture of elements" meaning is a later, largely Roman/Ovidian overlay. The Center for Hellenic Studies text and standard lexica confirm Hesiod's Chaos is located below earth and above Tartaros, and is a "gaping void."

**1.2 The Orphic theogonies.** This is where the early/late distinction is decisive.

- **Derveni papyrus** [ATTESTED, with heavy reconstruction]: UNESCO inscribed it (2015) as "the oldest surviving book in Europe"; the carbonized roll dates c. 340–320 BCE, and the Orphic poem it quotes and allegorizes was composed c. 420–410 BCE (some place the underlying poem earlier still, pre-500 BCE). The commentary was produced in the intellectual circle around Anaxagoras. In the Derveni poem, **Night (Nyx)** is primordial and gives birth to Ouranos, the first king; the succession then runs Ouranos → Kronos → Zeus, and Zeus **swallows** the phallus/first principle and re-creates the cosmos. **OPEN/CONTESTED**: Gábor Betegh (*The Derveni Papyrus*, Cambridge, 2004), and the argument in *Symbolae Osloenses* 80 (2005), hold that in the Derveni text **Zeus, not Dionysos, is central and Phanes may be entirely absent.** This directly undercuts any claim that the "standard Orphic egg-and-Phanes myth" is what the earliest Orphics taught.

- **Eudemian theogony** (named for Aristotle's pupil Eudemus, 4th c. BCE) [ATTESTED]: began with **Night (Nyx)** as first principle.

- **Hieronyman theogony** [CONDITIONAL — reconstructed from Damascius + Athenagoras, 2nd–6th c. CE]: the cosmic **egg** arises from primordial water and earth/matter; from it emerges **Protogonos** (identified with Phanes, and called Zeus and Pan).

- **Rhapsodic theogony** ("Orphic Rhapsodies," a late compilation) [CONDITIONAL — preserved mainly by Proclus and Damascius, 5th–6th c. CE]: begins with **Chronos** (Time — explicitly distinct from Kronos the Titan) as unaging first principle; Chronos generates **Aither** and **Chaos** (Chasma); Chronos fashions a cosmic **egg** in the Aither; from the egg springs **Phanes / Protogonos / Eros**, a bisexual four-headed winged creator-god (ram, bull, serpent, lion), also called **Metis**. Phanes is the first king; kingship passes Phanes → Nyx → Ouranos → Kronos → Zeus, and Zeus swallows Phanes to absorb and re-emit all creation. **Ananke (Necessity)** appears entwined with Chronos in the Hieronyman/Rhapsodic accounts.

**Crucial status flag** [OPEN/CONTESTED → borderline SPECULATIVE if projected backward]: M. L. West (*The Orphic Poems*, 1983) and Alberto Bernabé (the standard modern editor of the Orphic fragments) both stress that our richest Orphic material is preserved by **Neoplatonists who allegorized it to fit their own metaphysics**. Proclus's *Commentary on Plato's Timaeus* (c. 440 CE; ed./trans. Tarrant, Cambridge, 2007) is our single richest conduit for Neoplatonic Orphic material, repeatedly quoting the "writings of Orpheus." (A frequently-cited figure that Proclus preserves ~176 Orphic references, 139 in the Timaeus commentary, traces to Brisson; I could not independently verify the exact counts and flag them as secondhand.) Therefore the tidy "Chronos → egg → Phanes" system should be treated as a **Hellenistic-to-late-antique systematization**, whose earliest secure kernel is only "a first principle + a cosmic egg + a firstborn light/creator," not the full iconography. Do not present the four-headed Phanes as "pre-Titanic Greek belief c. 700 BCE."

**1.3 Pherecydes of Syros (fl. mid-6th c. BCE).** [ATTESTED — fragments in Damascius, Proclus, Clement, Maximus of Tyre; DK 7]
One of the earliest Greek prose cosmogonies, and the one with an explicitly **geometric/textile** structure. Three principles exist eternally: **Zas** (Ζάς, a form of Zeus), **Chthoniē** (Χθονίη, the chthonic/earth power), and **Chronos** (Χρόνος, Time). Chronos produces from his own seed fire, wind/air, and water, distributed into "recesses" (the title *Pentemychos*, "five recesses"). Zas, "when about to create, turns into **Eros**" (Proclus). Zas weaves a great **embroidered cloth (pharos)** on which he depicts **Gē (Earth) and Ogenos (Ōkeanos)** and their halls, and presents it as a wedding gift, draping it over a **"winged oak" (hypópteros drûs)** — an image with no precedent in earlier Greek tradition. This is, per the *Journal of Hellenic Studies* (Cambridge Core), "apparently the first Hellenic articulation of the view that the cosmos is a divinely designed artefact." The winged-oak-plus-embroidered-mantle is the earliest Greek cosmogony that is literally a *constructed, decorated object* — relevant to the user's "build the structure" interest, though the fragments do not give metric proportions.

**1.4 The Homeric alternative and minor cosmogonies.**
- **Homer, *Iliad* 14** [EXACT — primary text]: the "Deception of Zeus" twice has Hera call **Okeanos "genesis of the gods" and Tethys "mother"** (Il. 14.201, 14.246, 14.302), implying a tradition in which **Okeanos and Tethys**, not Ouranos and Gaia, are the primeval parents. West and others take this as a genuine alternative archaic stratum, possibly influenced by Near Eastern (Mesopotamian Apsû/Tiamat) water-origin cosmogony.
- **Alcman (7th c. BCE)** [ATTESTED — scholia fragment]: a cosmogony in which primordial unformed matter is ordered; the sequence names **Thetis** (read as *Thesis*, "creation/establishment"), then **Poros** ("contriver/passage," ~ path/means) and **Tekmor** ("ordinance/marker"), and **Skotos** (darkness). Heavily reconstructed but genuinely archaic.
- **Epimenides (6th c. BCE)** [ATTESTED, fragmentary]: began from **Aer (Air)** and **Nyx (Night)**, producing Tartaros and then an egg.
- **Musaeus** [ATTESTED, fragmentary]: a legendary Orphic-adjacent figure to whom theogonic verses were attributed.
- **Eudemus' account**: the Peripatetic Eudemus of Rhodes catalogued these theogonies, which is why later doxographers (and Damascius) could compare Night-first, Chronos-first, and Okeanos-first schemes.

**Which are genuinely pre-Titanic strata vs. later systematization?** [My assessment, grounded in the sources]
- Genuinely archaic (8th–6th c. BCE) **kernels**: Hesiod's Chaos/Gaia/Tartaros/Eros; the Homeric Okeanos-Tethys origin; Pherecydes' triad; Alcman's Thesis/Poros/Tekmor; the *idea* of a cosmic egg (Epimenides, early Orphic).
- Later systematization (Hellenistic–Neoplatonic): the full **Rhapsodic** Chronos→egg→four-headed-Phanes→Zeus-swallows-Phanes narrative, and the neat triadic metaphysics Damascius and Proclus impose.

### PART 2 — VEDIC COSMOGONY

General dating [ATTESTED]: Following Michael Witzel (1995), the Rigveda is dated c. 1500–1200 BCE, with codification in the early Kuru kingdom c. 1200–1000 BCE; the "family books" (Maṇḍalas 2–7) are the oldest core (~38% of the text), while **Maṇḍalas 1 and 10 are the latest portion, composed shortly before redaction.** All the famous cosmogonic hymns are in Maṇḍala 10. This "late Book 10 problem" means the philosophical cosmogonies are *not* the earliest Vedic layer — a direct parallel to the Greek situation, where the most systematic cosmogonies are the latest.

**2.1 Rigveda 10.129 — Nāsadīya Sūkta ("Hymn of Creation").** [EXACT content]
Opens: *nāsad āsīn no sad āsīt tadānīm* — "Then there was neither non-being (asat) nor being (sat); no air, no heaven beyond." There was no death nor immortality, no sign of night or day; **"That One" (tad ekam)** breathed windlessly by its own impulse. Darkness hidden in darkness, all was undifferentiated water (salila); the One arose through **tapas** (heat/austerity). Then came **kāma** (desire) — "the first seed of mind" — identified by seers as the bond between being and non-being. The famous closing verses:
- v.6: **"The gods came afterwards, with the creation of this universe. Who then knows whence it has arisen?"**
- v.7: "Whence this creation arose — whether it formed itself or did not — **he who looks down on it from the highest heaven, only he knows — or perhaps he does not know.**"
This closing is one of the earliest attested expressions of principled **agnosticism/skeptical inquiry** in world literature (Jamison–Brereton, *The Rigveda*, Oxford 2014; the verse is the one Carl Sagan popularized in *Cosmos*).

**2.2 Rigveda 10.121 — Hiraṇyagarbha Sūkta ("Golden Womb/Embryo").** [EXACT + comparative note]
In the beginning arose **Hiraṇyagarbha** (the "golden germ/embryo/womb"), the one lord of all that came to be, who established earth and sky. Every verse but the last ends with the refrain **"kásmai devā́ya havíṣā vidhema"** — "to which/what god shall we offer worship?" — the interrogative *ka* ("who?") later reified as a name of the creator; the final verse names **Prajāpati**. (The "golden womb" motif first appears in the Viśvakarman hymn RV 10.82.) The Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa and Upaniṣads develop Hiraṇyagarbha into the cosmic egg that splits into heaven and earth. This is the closest Vedic analogue to the Orphic egg (see Part 3).

**2.3 Rigveda 10.90 — Puruṣa Sūkta ("Hymn of the Cosmic Man").** [EXACT + stratification flag]
The cosmic **Puruṣa** has a thousand heads, eyes, feet; he pervades the earth and extends beyond it "by ten fingers." The gods perform a **sacrifice using the Puruṣa as the victim** ("With the sacrifice the gods sacrificed to the sacrifice"); from his dismembered body the world is generated: the **moon from his mind, the sun from his eye, Indra and Agni from his mouth, Vāyu from his breath; the sky from his head, earth from his feet, the four directions from his ear.** Verse 12 derives the four **varṇas**: Brahmin from the mouth, Rājanya/Kṣatriya from the arms, Vaiśya from the thighs, Śūdra from the feet. **OPEN/CONTESTED**: many scholars (going back to Max Müller) regard the varṇa verse and possibly the whole hymn as a **late interpolation**, added to give theological sanction to social hierarchy in the Kuru polity; Jamison–Brereton treat RV 10.90 as one of the latest hymns.

**2.4 Related figures and Brāhmaṇa material.** [ATTESTED]
- **Prajāpati** ("Lord of Creatures") becomes *the* creator in the Brāhmaṇas, often via **tapas** and self-sacrifice; the **agnicayana** fire-altar ritual reenacts Prajāpati's self-dismemberment and reconstitution (see geometry, Part 4).
- **Tvaṣṭṛ** — the divine artificer/fashioner (shaper of forms and of the *vajra*).
- **Viśvakarman** ("all-maker," RV 10.81–82) — a demiurge/architect figure; RV 10.82 places the "primeval germ" (golden womb) on the navel of the unborn.
- **Aditi / Dakṣa** (RV 10.72) — a famously paradoxical mutual generation: "**From Aditi Dakṣa was born, and from Dakṣa Aditi**" — an explicit circular cosmogony.

### PART 3 — THE COMPARATIVE / PROTO-INDO-EUROPEAN LAYER (AND ITS LIMITS)

**3.1 What comparative linguistics actually establishes.** [EXACT for the cognate sets]
The sky-father theonym is a secure reconstruction: PIE **\*Dyēus ph₂tēr** ("Sky-Father") → Vedic **Dyáuṣ Pitṛ́**, Greek **Zeus (patēr)** (gen. *Diós*, vocative *Zeû páter*), Latin **Jupiter/Diēspiter**, Illyrian **Dei-pátrous**, Palaic **Tiyaz papaz**, Scythian **Papaios**. The common root **\*dyeu-** ("to shine, sky, day") also yields **\*deiwós** "god" → Latin *deus*, Sanskrit *devá*, Old Norse *Týr*. These are among the most robust results in historical linguistics (West, *Indo-European Poetry and Myth*, 2007, p. 171). Also reasonably secure: dawn-goddess **\*H₂éusōs** (Greek *Eos*, Vedic *Uṣas*, Latin *Aurora*) and thunder-god **\*Perkʷunos** (Vedic *Parjanya*, Slavic *Perun*). Note that in the Rigveda Dyaus is already a faded, mostly-formulaic figure (usually in the dual *Dyāvā-Pṛthivī*, "Heaven-and-Earth"), whereas Zeus became the active sky-king — so cognate *name* does not mean identical *role*.

**3.2 M. L. West, *Indo-European Poetry and Myth* (2007).** [ATTESTED — peer-reviewed monograph]
West assembles poetic-formulaic parallels (e.g., "imperishable fame," *kléos áphthiton* ≈ Vedic *śrávas ákṣitam*; the earth-and-sky parents; the waters of origin). West is generally *cautious*: he accepts secure linguistic cognates and some formulae, while flagging that much thematic overlap could be typological (independent) rather than genetic.

**3.3 Bruce Lincoln's Manu/Yemo first-sacrifice.** [CONDITIONAL — broadly accepted reconstruction]
Lincoln (1975, "The Indo-European Myth of Creation") and, independently, Jaan Puhvel, reconstruct a founding myth: the first priest **\*Manu** ("Man") sacrifices his twin **\*Yemo** ("Twin"), and from Yemo's dismembered body the world (and the social classes) are made; a third figure, **\*Trito**, recovers stolen cattle (warrior-function charter). Reflexes: Vedic **Manu & Yama**; Iranian **Yima**; Norse **Ymir** (world made from his body); Roman **Romulus & Remus** (Remus < \*Yemo). This motif has been "generally accepted among scholars" since the 1970s (Mallory, Adams, Anthony, Puhvel, and in part West support it) — **but there is no consensus on the exact reconstruction**, and the Puruṣa Sūkta (RV 10.90) is the clearest Indic reflex of the "body-of-the-victim becomes the cosmos" pattern.

**3.4 Dumézil's trifunctional hypothesis — and the serious criticism.** [OPEN/CONTESTED, tilting toward discredited-as-overreach]
Georges Dumézil proposed that PIE ideology was structured in three "functions" — sovereignty/priesthood, warrior, producer/fertility — reflected across pantheons (e.g., Vedic Mitra-Varuṇa / Indra / Aśvins). **Criticisms the user should know:**
- **Methodological**: critics argue Dumézil imposed structural symmetry and cherry-picked, treating widely-scattered similar phenomena as if they must reduce to a single originary point (Lincoln's "fallacy of misplaced concreteness").
- **Political**: Bruce Lincoln (*Death, War, and Sacrifice*, 1991), and separately Arnaldo Momigliano and Carlo Ginzburg, argued Dumézil's 1930s formulation was inflected by sympathies with the far right / French fascist milieu (Action Française). This is itself contested and does not by itself refute the structural claim, but it is part of the scholarly record.
- **Net position**: The trifunctional hypothesis is today treated as, at best, a heuristic for *some* Indo-Iranian and Roman material, not a demonstrated feature of PIE society. Do not present it as established fact.

**3.5 The cosmic-egg parallel (Orphic vs. Hiraṇyagarbha): what specialists actually conclude.** [OPEN/CONTESTED]
This is exactly where the user must resist over-reading. The honest state of the question:
- The Vedic Hiraṇyagarbha (RV 10.121, late) and the Orphic egg (early kernel; late elaboration) are **both real**, and superficially similar (a golden/luminous egg or germ from primordial water, splitting/hatching to yield the ordered cosmos and a firstborn creator).
- **But**: (a) the world-egg motif is near-universal (it appears independently in Egyptian, Chinese, Finnish, Polynesian myth), which is strong evidence for **independent innovation / typological universality** rather than shared PIE inheritance; (b) there is **no secure PIE etymology** linking Hiraṇyagarbha and Phanes/Protogonos, unlike the airtight \*Dyēus case; (c) the *developed* Orphic egg is attested only in late-antique sources roughly contemporary with or later than Indian-contact horizons, so **later diffusion/parallel Hellenistic-Indian exchange cannot be excluded**. West notes the egg but does not treat it as a secured PIE cosmogonic inheritance. **Verdict: a genuine typological parallel, NOT a demonstrated common inheritance. Treat "the Orphic egg proves an Indo-European cosmic egg" as an over-reading.**

**3.6 Where 19th-century comparative mythology overreached — specific discredited moves to avoid.** [ATTESTED history of scholarship]
- **Max Müller's "solar mythology"**: Müller argued nearly all Indo-European myths were allegories of solar/dawn phenomena, and that mythology as a whole was a "**disease of language**" (myths arising from misunderstood, faded metaphors, especially old names for the sun). This was demolished already in the late 19th century — famously parodied by Andrew Lang, who "proved" by Müller's own method that Müller himself was a solar myth. **Do not** explain gods as "really the sun."
- **Etymological free-association**: deriving deep myth-identities from loose sound-resemblance (the trap the "Zas/Zeus" and "Chaos" material can fall into) rather than regular sound-law cognation.
- **Pan-Aryan diffusionism**: assuming any parallel motif descends from a single Aryan Urvolk.
- **What the modern discipline retains**: rigorous cognate theonyms and formulae established by regular sound laws (\*Dyēus, \*H₂éusōs, \*kléos áphthiton), and cautiously, a few narrative motifs (Manu/Yemo, the serpent-slaying/Trito). **What it rejects**: solar-allegory reductionism, Müller's "disease of language," and strong Dumézilian trifunctional determinism.

### PART 4 — THE CONSTRUCTIBLE GEOMETRY (buildable specifications)

#### 4.1 ŚULBA-SŪTRAS — Vedic altar geometry

**Texts and dates** [ATTESTED]: Baudhāyana (c. 800 BCE, oldest and best-preserved), Mānava (c. 750 BCE), Āpastamba (c. 600 BCE), Kātyāyana (c. 200 BCE). *śulba* = "cord/rope"; the constructions use a marked cord (with *nyañcana*, marks) stretched between pegs. These are appendices (part of the Kalpa/Śrauta-sūtras) to the Yajurveda.

**(a) The "Pythagorean" diagonal rule** [EXACT — Baudhāyana Śulba-sūtra i.48]:
> "The rope [stretched along] the diagonal of a rectangle produces the [area] that the vertical and horizontal sides produce [together]."

Baudhāyana i.49 then lists rectangles giving integer diagonals — five **primitive Pythagorean triples** plus one derived: **(3,4,5), (12,5,13), (15,8,17), (7,24,25), (12,35,37)**, and the non-primitive **(15,36,39)** (the last included because it underlies the mahāvedi altar). This statement precedes Pythagoras by roughly two centuries. [Seidenberg 1962; Plofker 2009]

**(b) √2 approximation** [EXACT — Baudhāyana i.61–62 / Āpastamba i.6]:
> *"pramāṇaṃ tṛtīyena vardhayet tac caturthenātmacatustriṃśonena saviśeṣaḥ"* — "Increase the measure by its third, and that [third] by its own fourth diminished by its own thirty-fourth part; [that is the diagonal] with a small excess (*saviśeṣa*)."

$$\sqrt{2} \approx 1 + \tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1}{3\cdot4} - \tfrac{1}{3\cdot4\cdot34} = \tfrac{577}{408} = 1.4142156\ldots$$

Correct value 1.4142135…; accurate to 5 decimal places. (Extending the pattern one more term, 665857/470832, gives ~13-decimal accuracy.) Note [per Staal/Seidenberg]: this precision is far beyond any ritual need — Seidenberg calls it "erudition."

**(c) Circling the square (square → equal-area circle)** [EXACT construction; approximate result — Baudhāyana i.58]:
Procedure: given a square of side $2a$ with centre O, take half the diagonal ($a\sqrt2$) and swing it from the centre out across the midpoint of a side; the desired circle's **radius = the side-half plus one third of the projecting excess**:

$$r = a + \tfrac{1}{3}\big(a\sqrt{2}-a\big) = \frac{(2+\sqrt2)\,a}{3}.$$

For the unit square this gives circle area ≈ 1.01725 (about 1.7% too large; implied π ≈ 3.0883). [arXiv "Cognition of the circle in ancient India"; Kātyāyana repeats it verbatim.] The **Mānava** and **Maitrāyaṇīya** versions (radius ≈ 9/16 × side) are actually more accurate (~0.5% error).

**(d) Squaring the circle (circle → equal-area square)** [EXACT construction; approximate — Baudhāyana i.59–60]:
The inverse rule effectively takes side $s = \tfrac{7}{8}d$ or uses a fractional rule on the diameter $d$; values of π implied across the sutras range roughly 3.004–3.088.

**(e) Area-preserving transformation** [EXACT — the organizing principle]: The whole system is built on transforming one figure into another **of equal area but different shape** (square↔rectangle, square↔circle, combining/subtracting squares). This is the ritual requirement — an altar could change shape but never area (except the deliberate stepwise enlargement, below).

**(f) The falcon altar (śyenaciti) and agnicayana brick arithmetic** [ATTESTED — Staal, *Agni*, 1983; Seidenberg 1962; Kak; Price; Sen & Bag 1983]:
- **Shape**: a great bird (falcon/*śyena*) in flight — a square (or shaped) **body**, a **head**, two **wings**, and a **tail**; built to "fly the sacrificer to heaven."
- **Layers**: **5 courses (citi)**; layer height 6 aṅgulas (total 30 aṅgulas ≈ 2 ft); layers 1/3/5 share one design, layers 2/4 another so that "the joints between bricks in successive layers never coincide vertically" (Staal).
- **Bricks per layer**: **200 shaped bricks per layer → ~1,000 for the physical falcon.** Staal (*Agni*): *"The altar is built in five layers. Each layer has exactly two hundred bricks. The total area of each layer is exactly seven and a half square units of the standard measure… The bricks come in fourteen standard shapes."*
- **Special (yajuṣmatī, mantra-consecrated) bricks**: **396 total** (Kak), distributed by layer **98 + 41 + 71 + 47 + 138**, "explained as 360 days of the year and the additional 36 days of the intercalary month."
- **The 10,800 figure**: a **symbolic count** — per Kak, *"The number of ordinary bricks equals 10,800 which equals the number of muhūrtas in a year (1 day = 30 muhūrtas)"* (30 × 360 = 10,800), a symbolism drawn from the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa; these are the *lokampṛṇa* ("space-filling") bricks. **Do NOT conflate this with the ~1,000 physically-laid shaped bricks** — popular and nationalist sources routinely (and wrongly) state "the falcon altar has 10,800 bricks." Specialists (Staal, Price) give 200/layer × 5.
- **Area**: **7½ square puruṣas**, where **1 puruṣa = 120 aṅgulas** (Baudhāyana i.21 — the height of the sacrificer with arms raised; 1 aratni = 24 aṅgulas = ⅕ puruṣa; 1 aṅgula ≈ ¾ inch).
- **Enlargement rule** [EXACT — Baudhāyana ii.1–6, 12]: at each successive annual performance the altar is enlarged **by one square puruṣa while keeping the same shape**, running 7½ → 8½ → 9½ → … → 101½ square puruṣas (95 steps). To enlarge by $q$ square puruṣas, replace the unit square with a square of area $1 + \tfrac{2q}{15}$ (new unit side $=\sqrt{1 + 2q/15}$ puruṣa). This area-preserving-yet-scaling problem, per **Seidenberg**, is a plausible ritual motive for discovering the diagonal (Pythagorean) rule.
- **Caturaśra ("square falcon," Mānava Śulba-sūtra)** [ATTESTED — buildable spec, via UPenn/Patrick George]: body square = **240 × 240 aṅgulas (= 2 puruṣas per side)**; brick inventory per layer — layers 1,3,5: **110 bricks of 30×30 aṅgula, 85 of 12×12, 5 of 12×6**; layers 2,4: **110 of 30×30, 75 of 12×12, 10 of 12×6, 5 of 18×12** — each layer summing to 200.

*Caveat on Seidenberg's chronology* [OPEN/CONTESTED]: Seidenberg ("The Ritual Origin of Geometry," *Archive for History of Exact Sciences* 1, 1962, pp. 488–527) argued a common ritual origin of geometry older than both Greece and Babylon (pre-1700 BCE). This strong dating has been **weakened** by later work (e.g., Eleanor Robson's dating of the Babylonian tablet Plimpton 322 to c. 1800 BCE); the structural observations survive, the deep-common-source dating is not consensus.

#### 4.2 PLATO, *Timaeus* — the five regular solids and the world-soul

**(a) Elements ↔ regular solids** [EXACT — Timaeus 53c–56c]:

| Solid | Faces | Element | Rationale (Plato) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tetrahedron | 4 triangles | fire | sharpest, most penetrating |
| Octahedron | 8 triangles | air | intermediate |
| Icosahedron | 20 triangles | water | most nearly spherical, flowing |
| Cube | 6 squares | earth | most stable; only one that tiles space |
| **Dodecahedron** | **12 pentagons** | **cosmos/aether** | "**the god used it for the whole**" (55c), for "arranging the constellations on the whole heaven" |

**(b) Elemental triangles** [EXACT — 53c–54b]: The faces of the first three solids reduce to a single elemental right triangle — the **half-equilateral (30-60-90) scalene triangle with sides 1 : √3 : 2**; the cube's faces reduce to the **45-45-90 isosceles right triangle**. Because fire/air/water share the same 30-60-90 building block, they can **transform into one another** (by rearranging triangles); earth (built from a *different* triangle) cannot transmute into the others. Each equilateral face is built from **six** of the 30-60-90 triangles (Plato's choice; two would suffice geometrically).

**(c) The world-soul numbers (the "lambda")** [EXACT — Timaeus 35b–36b]: The Demiurge divides the soul-stuff along two geometric progressions, the powers of 2 and 3:

$$1,\;2,\;3,\;4,\;9,\;8,\;27$$

i.e. the doubles **1, 2, 4, 8** and triples **1, 3, 9, 27** (arranged as the "lambda" Λ with 1 at the apex). He then fills each interval with **two means**:
- **harmonic mean** $H = \dfrac{2ab}{a+b}$ and **arithmetic mean** $A = \dfrac{a+b}{2}$.
- Between 1 and 2: harmonic 4/3, arithmetic 3/2 — giving the musical fourth (4/3), fifth (3/2), and their ratio the **whole tone 9/8**.
- The remaining gaps are filled with intervals of **9/8** (tones), leaving a **leftover interval (leimma) of 256/243** — the Pythagorean diatonic semitone. This constructs a diatonic scale spanning four octaves plus a major sixth.

The soul-fabric is then split lengthwise into two strips bent into the **circle of the Same** (celestial equator, undivided) and the **circle of the Different** (the ecliptic, divided into the seven planetary orbits at intervals 1,2,3,4,8,9,27).

**(d) Euclid, *Elements* Book XIII** [EXACT — provable]: Book XIII constructs all five regular solids **inscribed in a sphere**, computes each edge relative to the sphere's radius, and its final proposition (XIII.18) proves that **no other regular solids exist** (the vertex-angle-sum < 360° condition permits exactly five: {3,3}, {3,4}, {3,5}, {4,3}, {5,3}). Proclus reports the view (defended later by Andreas Speiser) that the *culmination* of the Elements is precisely this construction of the five Platonic solids. Theaetetus (c. 417–369 BCE) is credited with the first systematic treatment and the uniqueness proof.

#### 4.3 ANAXIMANDER (c. 610–546 BCE)

[ATTESTED core / CONDITIONAL numbers — via Hippolytus *Refutatio*, ps.-Plutarch, Aetius; only one sentence of Anaximander's own words survives]
- **Apeiron** (τὸ ἄπειρον, "the boundless/indefinite"): the eternal, ageless, unlimited source from which the *opposites* (hot/cold, wet/dry) separate out and worlds arise and perish. (Firmly attributed.)
- **Drum/column-shaped earth**: the earth is a **cylinder (stone column) whose height is one-third its diameter** (3 : 1 ratio, diameter : height); we live on the flat top. It stays put because it is **equidistant from everything** (no need for support) — a landmark argument. [Aetius, Hippolytus]
- **Celestial wheels** [CONDITIONAL — the Diels–Tannery reconstruction]: the heavenly bodies are rings/wheels of fire encased in mist with vent-holes ("bellows-nozzles") through which fire shows. The sources give the **sun wheel = 27 (or 28) × earth**, **moon wheel = 18 (or 19) ×**. Tannery and Diels reconstructed the full scheme as concentric radii in **earth-diameters: stars/planets 9 (–10), moon 18 (–19), sun 27 (–28)** — the ×9 series, with each wheel one earth-diameter thick (hence the paired inner/outer numbers). **Status flag**: the *ratios 9/18/27 are a modern reconstruction* extrapolating the innermost "stars" ring, which the doxography does not state numerically; Hippolytus gives 27 for the sun, Aetius 28. Do not present "9/18/27" as a verbatim ancient quotation. The recurrence of **9** (and its speculative link to "new"/"far," énnea~néos) is a much-discussed interpretive layer (Robert Hahn), not established fact.

#### 4.4 PYTHAGOREAN MATERIAL

[OPEN/CONTESTED attribution — Burkert]
- **Tetractys**: the triangular array of 1+2+3+4 = **10** (the "perfect number"), by which Pythagoreans reportedly swore oaths (Iamblichus). Andrew Gregory judges the Pythagoras–tetractys link "probably genuine."
- **Honest attribution flag** [per Walter Burkert, *Lore and Science in Ancient Pythagoreanism*, 1972 — the field-defining study]: Pythagoras himself wrote nothing; he is best understood as a **charismatic religious/political teacher** concerned with the soul and ritual, and the **mathematical/number-cosmology was largely the innovation of Philolaus of Croton (c. 470–385 BCE)**, the earliest Pythagorean with genuine surviving fragments. Much "Pythagorean mathematics" (including the neat number-metaphysics and possibly the "Pythagorean theorem" attribution) was retrojected onto Pythagoras — some already in Plato's Academy (Speusippus, Xenocrates), some by later Neopythagoreans/Neoplatonists via forged treatises. **So: treat "Pythagoras discovered/taught X" as late tradition unless X is anchored in Philolaus.**

#### 4.5 KALACHAKRA MANDALA CONSTRUCTION

**Dating** [ATTESTED — critical scholarship]: The **Kālacakra Tantra** (properly the *Laghu-* or abridged tantra) and its master commentary the **Vimalaprabhā** ("Stainless Light," ~12,000 lines of Sanskrit, attributed to Puṇḍarīka; see Vesna Wallace, *The Inner Kālacakratantra*, Oxford, 2001) appear in India in the **early 11th century CE**. The Abridged Tantra itself (I.27) fixes the first sixty-year *prabhava* (*rab-byung*) cycle at **1027 CE**, the date the system was **introduced to Tibet** (via the Kashmiri paṇḍita Somanātha and translator Dro Lotsāwa, founding the Dro lineage); John Newman dates the Vimalaprabhā to c. 1027 CE. **This is Vajrayāna Buddhism, ~1,800–2,500 years younger than the Rigveda and ~1,500 years younger than Plato.** It is emphatically NOT a Vedic or Indo-European cosmogonic document.

**Constructible grid** [ATTESTED — via the Vajrāvalī of Abhayākaragupta and Tibetan iconometric manuals; measured in *mus* (grid units)]:
- The mandala is the **ground-plan of a five-storey palace** (levels = body, speech, mind, pristine-consciousness, great bliss), square, with **four T-shaped gates (toraṇa)** at the cardinal directions.
- The layout proceeds by nested **concentric squares** (final lines, not construction lines): the outer beam-line of the circle of great bliss, and the parapet lines of the **mind, speech, and body** palaces — exponentially-proportioned squares nested within each other.
- The body-mandala platform is often given as **200 × 200 armspans**, with the speech-mandala on a 100-armspan platform above it, and the mind mandala above that (the "exponential" nesting).
- **Outer rings**: from the innermost square outward, bands of specified widths are measured out — per the Vajrāvalī, successive bands of **12, 24, 24, 24, 12, 24 mus** for the perimeters of **earth, water, fire, wind, space, and the garland of light (vajra-fire)**.
- **Total diameter = 624 mus** (the final outer circle), completing the construction.
- The four quadrants are colour-coded by direction/element (black/E, red/S, white/W, yellow/N — matching the four faces of Kalachakra), resting on discs of earth/water/fire/wind; **722 deities** total in the full three-dimensional palace (536 in the body mandala).

**Category-conflation flag**: squares-within-circles with four gates is the *general* Indian mandala/vāstu grammar, which does have deep Indian roots — but the **specific Kalachakra system is 11th-c. Buddhist**. Using its iconography to illustrate "the Vedas" (see Part 5) mixes three distinct strata (Vedic ritual geometry c. 800 BCE; classical Hindu; Tibetan Buddhist tantra c. 1027 CE).

The mandala is a marketable pan-"Eastern spirituality" signifier, but it is **iconographically wrong for the contents** — a textbook example of exactly the kind of category conflation the user wants to avoid (analogous to putting a Tibetan thangka on a book of Homeric hymns).

## Caveats (unsparing)

1. **There is no single "pre-Titanic Greek cosmogony."** Anyone who presents one clean sequence is smoothing over Hesiodic vs. Orphic vs. Homeric vs. Pherecydean divergence. Eudemus already knew they disagreed.
2. **The developed Orphic system is late.** The four-headed Phanes, the egg, and the Zeus-swallows-Phanes narrative are secured only in Damascius/Proclus/Athenagoras (2nd–6th c. CE). The Derveni papyrus — the *earliest* witness (poem c. 420–410 BCE) — may lack Phanes entirely and centres on Zeus. Projecting the Rhapsodic system back to c. 700 BCE is unwarranted.
3. **All the famous Vedic cosmogonies are late (Rigveda Maṇḍala 10).** They are roughly contemporary with, or later than, the family-book core, and 10.90's varṇa verse is widely held to be interpolated. "The Vedas' creation account" is really "one late layer of the Rigveda."
4. **The cosmic-egg Greek↔Vedic parallel is NOT proven common inheritance.** It is most defensibly a near-universal typological motif (independent innovation), with later diffusion not excludable. The airtight comparative result is the *linguistic* \*Dyēus set, not any cosmogonic narrative.
5. **Dumézil's trifunctionalism and Müller's solar mythology are cautionary tales, not tools.** Avoid solar-allegory reductionism, sound-alike etymologizing, and strong trifunctional determinism.
6. **Two different Vedic altar brick-counts circulate and are routinely conflated.** ~1,000 physical shaped bricks (200/layer × 5) vs. 10,800 symbolic (= muhūrtas of the year, the *lokampṛṇa* count). Popular and nationalist sources frequently state the 10,800 as the physical count; specialists (Staal, Price, Kak) do not.
7. **Anaximander's 9/18/27 is a reconstruction.** The doxography gives 27/28 (sun) and 18/19 (moon); the innermost "9" for the stars and the clean ×9 ratio are Diels–Tannery extrapolation.
8. **"Pythagorean" mathematics is largely Philolaus and later.** Per Burkert, Pythagoras himself probably contributed no mathematics; attributions are mostly retrojection.
9. **The Kalachakra mandala is chronologically and religiously wrong for a "Vedas" cover.** It is 11th-c. CE Tibetan Vajrayāna Buddhism. The cover is a marketing choice, not a scholarly statement — and is itself an object lesson in the category conflation to avoid.
10. **Source-quality note**: I have leaned on primary texts in translation (Hesiod via CHS; Homer *Iliad* 14; RV 10.129/10.121/10.90; Timaeus 35b–56c; Baudhāyana i.48–62) and peer-reviewed/specialist secondary work (West 2007; Bernabé; Betegh 2004; Burkert 1972; Seidenberg 1962; Staal 1983; Plofker 2009; Kak; Diels–Tannery; Newman; Wallace 2001; Witzel 1995). Where I used encyclopedic or popular pages (Grokipedia, fan wikis, blogs), I treated them only as pointers and flagged contested numbers. Items still **unverified against a specialist source** (do not build as EXACT): the exact falcon wing/tail *aratni*-increment sutra wording, the mahāvedi "972-square-unit" area, the Brisson 176/139 Proclus counts, and the translator/source-language of the Dodoni *Vedes*.
