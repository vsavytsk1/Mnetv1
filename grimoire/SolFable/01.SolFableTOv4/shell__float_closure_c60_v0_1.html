The Closure — the float path on a buckyball

A path has two ends. Close it into a sphere and Euler charges you exactly twelve pentagons — the same twelve at six bits and at sixty-four.
exactcomputed liverefuted
The hit. A float with 1 sign bit, 4 exponent bits and 1 mantissa bit has exactly 60 finite bit patterns. And 60 = 20·3 with T = 3 = 1²+1·1+1² — a Loeschian number. That is C₆₀. The smallest float format that closes into an icosahedral Goldberg shell is the buckyball. Not an analogy. The integer arithmetic lands on it.

And it keeps going. The family E = 4, M odd gives V = 60 → 240 → 960 → 3840, each one V ↦ 4V. That is the WELD/chamfer lineage from THEA, arrived at from the opposite direction: adding two mantissa bits is one chamfer step.

IThe float64 fullerene — the cage that holds every double▶
exact Every even V ≥ 20 except 22 is the vertex count of some closed trivalent fullerene (Grünbaum–Motzkin). float64 has 2⁵³·2047 finite bit patterns — even, and vastly larger than 22. So the cage exists. Give it one vertex per bit pattern and the rest is forced:
V = 2^53 · 2047 one vertex per finite bit pattern E = 3V/2 trivalent F = V/2 + 2 Euler H = V/2 − 10 hexagons P = 12 forced, at every scale, forever
Nine quintillion hexagons. Twelve pentagons. The number of pentagons does not know how big the number system is.
IIThe buckyball format — and the WELD ladder, from the other side▶
exact An icosahedral Goldberg shell needs V = 20T with T = k²+kℓ+ℓ² — a Loeschian number. For a (1, E, M) format, V = 2^(M+1)·(2^E − 1). When does that land on a Goldberg shell? Computed live, exactly:
The smallest hit is E = 4, M = 1 → V = 60, T = 3. Six bits. C₆₀. And the whole E = 4 family with M odd walks V ↦ 4V:
M= 1 V = 60 T = 3 C60 the buckyball M= 3 V = 240 T = 12 C240 M= 5 V = 960 T = 48 C960 M= 7 V = 3840 T = 192 C3840 +2 mantissa bits = one chamfer
THEA's exact-ID WELD operator certifies precisely C₆₀ → C₂₄₀ → C₉₆₀ → C₃₈₄₀ with V ↦ 4V, P = 12, χ = 2. This page reached the same ladder from bit patterns, never having been told it existed. Two mantissa bits is one chamfer step.
design The correspondence is a counting identity, not a proof that the WELD graph and the float graph are isomorphic. They agree on V, E, F, P, H and χ. Whether the float successor relation can be realised as an edge set of the chamfer lineage at every level is open — for V = 60 it is verified below, by explicit Hamiltonian path.

IVThe growth law — hexagons are precision, pentagons are closure▶
exact Now watch what a bit buys. Add one mantissa bit: V doubles, H doubles, R grows by √2 — and P does not move.
formatVhexagonsPR / R₆₄
design mapping The reading: hexagons are precision; pentagons are closure. Hexagons tile flat and carry no curvature — you can have as many as you can afford, and each one is another bit of resolution. The twelve pentagons are the defect charge that lets the sheet close at all, 4π of it, paid once and never again. A number format that wants no ends pays twelve pentagons. A number format that wants dynamic range pays two ends. You are choosing which price to pay, not whether.
Stated as an analogy with its seams showing: the fullerene's uniform edge length is the geometric image of fixed-point arithmetic, and float's graded edges are what buy the exponent range. The cage counts the patterns; it does not encode their spacing.
VThe Loeschian gate — why float32 is not a buckyball▶
refuted The obvious hope — that the formats we actually use are Goldberg shells — is false, and the reason is a clean piece of number theory. T is Loeschian iff every prime ≡ 2 (mod 3) in its factorisation appears to an even power.
float32 fails on 17. Not on size, not on parity — on a single prime that is 2 mod 3 sitting there to the first power. And float64 fails earlier still: 2⁵³·2047 is not even divisible by 20, since 2047 = 23·89 carries no factor of 5.
computed live So float64 is a fullerene but not a Goldberg fullerene. It falls strictly between two shells of the golden ladder:
This is the honest shape of the result. One format lands exactly on the buckyball and its chamfer ladder; the ones in your CPU do not. The hit at six bits is real and the miss at thirty-two is real, and printing only the first would be the whole disease this cave was built against.
VIThe helix — the path was two-dimensional all along▶
exact Why a line can become a surface at all: nextafter carries. Step past the last mantissa value and the carry lands in the exponent field — mantissa wraps, exponent increments. The two fields are not independent axes; they are a coil, one turn per octave.
Set the morph to the middle and you are looking at it. The line was already wound; the sphere just closes the winding. float64 is a helix of 2047 turns with 4 503 599 627 370 496 nodes on each.
design The coil drawn above uses 15 turns for 60 nodes so the winding is visible at all. The real proportion — four quadrillion nodes per turn — cannot be drawn, only stated.
VIIThe honest boundary▶
exact The cage arithmetic for any V (BigInt, no floats). The 60-pattern count of the (1,4,1) format and T = 3. The E = 4, M odd ladder V = 60·4ʲ. The Loeschian criterion and float32's failure on 17. The helix identity 2·(2ᴱ−1)·2ᴹ = V. The C₆₀ coordinates from φ.

design Assigning one fullerene vertex per bit pattern is a choice, and the counting identity that follows is exact given that choice. "Hexagons are precision, pentagons are closure" is a reading, not a theorem. The helix drawing's turn count. Which Hamiltonian path the search happens to find — there are many.
not claimed That the float successor graph is isomorphic to any fullerene's edge graph. It is not: a fullerene is cubic, and a path is not. What is shown is that the float path embeds in the C₆₀ edge set as a Hamiltonian path — a strictly weaker and fully verified statement. Nothing here says IEEE 754 was designed with any of this in mind. It was not.
