WHY A TRANSFORMER IS PROVABLY HARD (funny graph math)

FORM A ⇄ FORM B : one collision-free path in C-space
rigid end = 0 DOF · path = 1 DOF (metamorphic)
pay ΔU every transition (no Allspark)
actuation work ∝ L⁴ vs actuators ∝ L³
Strip the toy to its skeleton: rigid panels (nodes) joined by hinges (edges).
Its possible poses form a configuration space; two useful regions —
FORM A and FORM B — are joined by a path that never lets a panel pass through
another. Elegant transforms move all at once because the whole fold is a
single coordinated motion: one degree of freedom.

Here's the provable tension: a form that holds its shape has zero internal
freedom; a form that can change shape needs at least one. The same structure
must be both, at different moments — a metamorphic mechanism. And because
the energy landscape has two wells with a hill between, you pay the barrier
ΔU to cross, every single time. The system is conservative; nothing
lowers the saddle for free.

Then Mama Nature sends her bill. Scale the machine up by L: its actuators
push with force ∝ L² over stroke ∝ L, so work ∝ L³ — but the work to heave
its own weight grows ∝ L⁴. The deficit grows with L. A toy folds for free;
a car-sized Transformer cannot move itself, and its joints crush under
their own weight (stress ∝ L). Hasbro bows to Mama Nature.

Honest: planning a path through a real high-dimensional C-space is
PSPACE-hard — no toy searches an "impossible fractal jungle" live. This shows
the one designed path, the well, and the wall, truthfully. χ = 2 · always.
