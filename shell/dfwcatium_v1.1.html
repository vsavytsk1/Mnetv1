HOW DO YOU LOCK A CAT BY ITS NOSE? (funny graph math)

minutiae → a 2-D constellation of points
hash geometric PAIRS → invariant tokens (== Shazam)
RANSAC inliers = one consistent transform
lock when captured bits ≥ 2·log₂(N) + margin
The coat looks like the fingerprint, but it melts — molt, light, pose,
left flank ≠ right flank. The nose is the real one: a bead-and-groove
pattern, unique and unchanging for life (proven in dogs, 2021; in cattle, 1922).

We treat its junctions as a constellation of points, exactly like Shazam
treats spectrogram peaks. We hash pairs of points into tokens that don't
care about rotation or scale, look them up in an inverted table, then run
RANSAC — keep only the matches that agree on a single geometry. That
agreement is the image-domain twin of Shazam's time-coherence spike.

How sharp a photo do you need? Enough to separate one nose from N of them.
The birthday bound says you need about 2·log₂(N)+margin bits — roughly
60 bits to be unique among a million cats, which a clean nose carries easily.
Blur the shot and the minutiae vanish; the captured bits fall below the line;
the lock won't fire. So the whole game is the capture loop: come closer,
hold steady, more light — until the nose gives up enough of itself.

Honest: real apps quote 95–99%, but the numbers are vendor claims on clean
images; wet, moving noses are the true bottleneck. This is a toy that shows
the shape of the problem — not a shipping matcher. χ = 2 · always.
