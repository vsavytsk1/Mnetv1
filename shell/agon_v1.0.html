honesty (K1–K7):
K1 a dynamical-systems teaching model, not a fitted cliometric simulation. It dramatizes one real thesis about Classical Greece; the curves are illustrative, the directions are the lesson.
K2 the agon is real: Greeks competed formally at drama, athletics, philosophy and rhetoric, and that fragmented, leisured, sea-linked competition genuinely turbo-charged output (Burckhardt's agonal culture; the leisure rested on slavery — modelled only as background wealth).
K3 the tragic coupling: rivalry drives BOTH culture AND internecine war. The Peloponnesian War + the plague of Athens are the archetype of the self-inflicted wound — here, high rivalry drains power on every front.
K4 two knobs do DIFFERENT jobs. rivalry makes the brilliance; unity (panhellenic feeling / durable federation) is what could let them keep the brilliance AND survive. They are independent on purpose — they were not culturally incapable, they were politically disunited.
K5 the hegemon wins by division: weakened, un-federated cities fall first, which lowers the collective shield and cascades (Philip at Chaeronea; then Rome). Unity raises the survival threshold — the buildable variable.
K6 "collapse" is a misnomer. The legacy counter keeps rising even at full conquest: Greek thought was absorbed by the Hellenistic kingdoms and Rome, which is why we still discuss it.
K7 names/positions are decorative. Tune to real scholarship before relying on any number. Stamp is a signature, not a claim.
