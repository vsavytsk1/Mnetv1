Too dry → the berm jams the chains and you PLOW TO A STOP. Too wet → the bed FLUIDIZES and the titan SINKS. Between them, a moving bubble of perfect sand the block rides — kept alive against evaporation + absorption. Push any slider to the limit; if it breaks, pull it back.

HOW DOES WATER MOVE A TITAN? (in pure graph math)

F = μ(w) · N   (you must out-pull the drag)
μ: 0.55 dry → 0.30 wet → rises again when flooded
w* = pour / (evaporation + absorption)
A sledge on sand is not fighting weight — it is fighting friction,
and friction is set by the sand's force-chain graph.

Dry sand jams: a berm of locked chains builds in front, drag runs away.
Pour the right water and capillary bridges — new edges — stiffen the
grains so no berm forms; the network carries the load smoothly and μ halves.
Pour too much and the bridges merge, the bed fluidizes, the graph
dissolves into a liquid, and the titan sinks. The sweet spot is an
operating point — a self-balancing attractor, the honest "energy well."

The block rides a moving bubble of perfect sand — a friendly wink at
a warp bubble, a travelling zone of altered medium. (A wink — not GR.
The real engine is grains and bridges.) You keep the bubble alive by
pouring faster than the desert can evaporate and absorb it away.

The Egyptians wrote this down and passed it on. That is the real magic.
