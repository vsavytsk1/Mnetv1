CHROMIUM v1.0 — the wave foundry
A microchip is a lattice of circles carved in chrome. Here each circle is a node; its 6 bonds are edges; the wave operator is the graph Laplacian L = D − A. On a lattice its eigenvectors are the Fourier modes — so we build any wave by summing graph eigenmodes: pick a target (focus, plane, Bessel, vortex, array) and it is re-synthesized from the lowest graph frequencies. More modes = finer wave, more detail = denser chip — both cost compute, to the Chromium limit. Drag to rotate.

A chip is just circles carved in chrome.
Make each circle a node, each bond an edge — now it's a graph.
The wave operator on that graph is the Laplacian L = D − A,
and here is the quiet miracle both of our searches confirmed:
on a lattice, the Laplacian's eigenvectors ARE the Fourier modes.
Graph Fourier and wave Fourier are the same mathematics — rigorous.

So to build any wave, you project it onto the lowest graph
frequencies and add them back. Few modes → a soft, blurred wave;
more modes → sharper — exactly the band-limit a real chip lives under.
A focus, a plane, a Bessel pencil, a vortex — all just sums of circles.

Every extra mode and every extra circle is paid for in compute,
until the Chromium limit — your browser's, reached long
before the real chrome-mask limit. Math is the base. You pay to see.
χ = 2 · always.
