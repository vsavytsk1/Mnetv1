SOUND, WATER & LUCK (in pure graph math)

F = μ(w) · N   — out-pull the drag
pull_eff = pullers · f · κ(beat)   — sing in time
Γ = A·ω² / g   — shake the chains loose (don't fluidize)
A sledge fights friction, and friction lives in the sand's force-chain graph.
Water grows capillary bridges that stiffen the grains so no berm forms —
the famous half-ing of μ. That part is settled physics.

Then the song. Its honest, documented job is coordination: a beat
locks 172 pullers into one instant of effort instead of a smear of half-pulls.
Sing in time and the effective pull climbs. Every hauling culture knew this.

Its second job is a maybe: a hard beat vibrates the ground, and
vibration shakes force-chains loose, lowering the sand's yield stress
(acoustic fluidization, Γ=Aω²/g). A little helps; too much and the graph
dissolves to liquid and the titan sinks. We have no proof Egyptians did this
— it's a what-if the sim lets you test, not a claim.

And the river was luck. The Nile's dilute alkaline water and fine silt
were a forgiving pairing; another bank's grain and chemistry might give you
no window at all. So some "secrets" were craft — and some were a good bank,
later blessed by an oracle who said "Ra did it," and then, crucially,
written down. The myth was wrong; the record is why the craft survived.
