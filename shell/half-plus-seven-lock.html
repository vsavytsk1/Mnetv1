Two birth instants, ten years apart, closing on a rule written in 1879. His age climbs at one second per second; her age climbs at one second per second; the line climbs at half that. The one number that never ticks
322,304,703 SI seconds
Every other figure on this page is in motion. Δ is not. It was fixed at 10:05 EEST on 15 July 2005 and it will read the same at both funerals: 3,730 days, 9 hours, 5 minutes — 10.2134 mean Gregorian years. Ratio of ages, though, is not fixed: it was 0.673 as of writing, hits 0.703 at the crossing, and crawls toward 1 forever without arriving. That's the whole phenomenon. The gap doesn't shrink; its share does.
Your clock says 322,304,700. The atomic clocks say 322,304,703. The difference is three leap seconds — inserted 1 Jan 1996, 1 Jul 1997, 1 Jan 1999 — and all three of them fall inside the gap. Earth's rotation slowed, the world held its clocks back three times, and every held-back second landed in the interval where he existed and she didn't. UTC−TAI has been pinned at −37 s since 2017-01-01; IERS Bulletin C 72 (6 July 2026) confirms no leap second through December 2026. TAI is assembled from roughly 450 clocks in 80-odd timing labs — close to your 480, and every one of them counting the same caesium transition at 9,192,631,770 Hz.

Your two S-curves, plotted

women's preferred partner age men's preferred partner age half your age + 7
You guessed the shape before you saw the data. Kenrick & Keefe (1992) found exactly this: women prefer men near their own age up to roughly ten years older and that preference barely moves across the lifespan; men's preferred partner age rises with a slope well under one, so the gap they want widens as they age — replicated in personal ads, marriage records from 1920s Arizona, a Philippine island, 17th-century Amsterdam, and a 29-country sample. Note where the chalk line sits: the 1879 formula runs parallel to the male curve and cuts straight across the female one. It was never a rule about couples. It was a rule about grooms. Curve shapes are schematic — the pattern is theirs, the exact coordinates are illustrative.

Where 10.21 years actually sits

Honest, not hidden

The crossing has a ±14 h 37 m band The millisecond above is exact given a definition of "year." Feed the formula a tropical year and it lands 04:32:36 UTC; a mean Gregorian year, 04:38:51; a Julian year, 07:10:03; the calendar's own answer — 14 anniversaries of 29 April — 19:10:03. Same day, 14 h 37 m of spread, because the rule's "+7" was written by a man who had never met a caesium atom. The moon clock's honest bar was ±2 minutes. This one's is fourteen hours, and it's the formula's fault, not the clock's.
And the rule itself has no error bar, because it has no data Frederick Locker-Lampson, Patchwork, 1879: a wife should be half her husband's age plus seven. Max O'Rell repeats it in 1901, in a chapter of advice to men who want to marry, alongside never marry a woman richer or taller than you. Note what it originally was: not a floor, an ideal — the target age of the bride. Under that reading this pair is never "correct" and then correct for one instant and then past it forever, which is a strange thing to build a countdown to, and exactly why it's worth building.
The 75,000-year story is off by a factor of twenty The healed femur is usually credited to Margaret Mead; nobody has found it in her writings, and it seems to enter circulation through a 2012 hospice book. The actual fossil record of care runs far deeper: Shanidar 1, a Neanderthal with a withered arm, healed skull fracture and blindness in one eye who lived for years afterwards, ~45–50 kya — and Dmanisi D3444, a Homo erectus skull that lost all its teeth and survived years toothless, ~1.77 million years ago. Care is older than us.
Selection didn't stop, either Lactase persistence swept Europe in the last 7,000–10,000 years. The Tibetan EPAS1 altitude variant is younger than agriculture. Malaria-resistance alleles are still under active selection right now. Contemporary cohorts show measurable directional selection on age at first birth. And the grandmother hypothesis argues the opposite of your premise: post-reproductive lifespan wasn't the thing that ended optimization, it was one of its products.
The two curves are real; the "biology" label is contested Kenrick & Keefe read the pattern as life-history strategy. Eagly & Wood read the same data as social structure, and they have a hard number on their side: the husband–wife gap falls by roughly 1.6 years per log point of GDP per capita across 89 countries, and the US gap went from 4.9 years in 1880 to 2.2 in 2022 without the genome changing at all. Whatever the curves are, they're at minimum elastic — which means the number on this page is a fact about two people, not a verdict from anywhere.
One finding that doesn't flatter anyone Lee & McKinnish: men with wives 1–3 years younger reported the highest initial marital satisfaction — and that advantage had fully dissipated by roughly the eighth year, after which differently-aged couples showed steeper declines and less resilience to financial and health shocks. It's a population average and it says nothing about any particular pair. Included because leaving it out would make this page dishonest.

Same trick as the moon clock: this runs on your machine, which is allowed to know what now is. The countdown above is real to the millisecond, and the thing it's counting down to is a Victorian aphorism about brides — so the precision is honest and the target is arbitrary, and knowing which is which is the entire point.
