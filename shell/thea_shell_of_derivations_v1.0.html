Two pages, one shell

The Dodecahedron of Open Questions has twelve pentagons and twenty points. THEA has twenty-four propositions and a dependency graph. Stand a THEA tower on each point and open the dodecahedron — truncate the dual icosahedron — and every point becomes a hexagon while every pentagon stays a pentagon. That is C₆₀.

12 pentagonsthe twelve open questions. Euler forces exactly twelve on any closed trivalent sphere; a pentagon is where the flat tiling cannot continue, so the curvature — the thing that will not close — lives there. 20 hexagonstwenty THEA towers, I to XX, each with its full formula stack typeset by the page’s own kernel. The colour is the card’s status. 60 nodesthe correlative nodes: each is the meeting of exactly two towers and one question, and it takes the weaker status of its two towers. the lightsection zero at the centre. Its two rays reach I and VII. Press trace and the light walks the dependency graph by depth; the light matrix advances once per beat and the HUD shows the shell T it has reached. gold bondsdependencies that lie flat on the shell — foundation and dependent are neighbouring hexagons. dashed arcsdependencies the shell cannot hold locally. Hexagon adjacency is the dodecahedral graph, three neighbours each; a card with more relations than that must send some of them over the top. These are the knots of the second kind; the placement that minimises them is annealed at load and receipted in the corner.
