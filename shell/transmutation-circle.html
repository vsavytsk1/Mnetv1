The Transmutation Circle

Every sigil is a graph: n points on a ring, each joined to the one k steps along. Raise the pact to inscribe it — but a single unbroken line only exists when gcd(n, k) = 1. Otherwise the figure shatters into rings, and nothing answers.

Why the rule holds. Joining every point to the one k steps away makes a 2-regular graph — a union of gcd(n, k) equal cycles. When that gcd is 1, all n points fall on a single cycle you can trace without lifting the pen (a Hamiltonian circuit); when it isn't, you get d interlocking rings, like {6/2} = two triangles. Complete Kₙ is traceable unbroken only when n is odd (every degree even → an Eulerian circuit). Same sky, same hours, same gold-by-day / silver-by-night as the almanac — just spent on geometry.
