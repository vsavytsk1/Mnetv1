Hard stop · the model does not run below 18
The sliders clamp at 18 and that's structural, not decorative. Here is the failure mode this model has, stated plainly: it converts "she's mature for her age" into a number, and that sentence is the single most common rationalisation in every exploitation case ever recorded. So the guardrail has to be built into the physics rather than bolted on.

The correction that saves your theory: a high π in a minor is a vulnerability marker, not a capacity certificate. The adultification literature is blunt about it — kids carrying adult responsibilities are alienated from peers and authority figures by their precocious knowledge, and presenting as more mature strips away protections rather than earning standing. The thing that makes them read 45 is the same thing that makes them easiest to take. Fast development is a scar, and a scar is not consent.

So: your model explains why a 14/21 gap feels catastrophic — it genuinely is the largest developmental distance on the whole board. It does not, and must not, run in the other direction to make one feel smaller. 18 is a floor, not a curve.

What's real here and what I made up

π is not a metaphor — it has a blood test DunedinPACE reports biological years elapsed per calendar year, built from 19 organ-system decline indicators tracked across two decades in the Dunedin 1972–73 birth cohort. Population centre 1.00, roughly 0.6 to 1.4 in practice. It runs fast in young adults with childhood adversity across five separate datasets, and in the E-Risk cohort the acceleration was already visible by age 18 in kids who grew up poor or were victimised early. Your "went through the most hard stuff" has a methylation signature. π is that number, borrowed.
The decaying rate is real; my curve is not Quoidbach, Gilbert & Wilson measured personality, values and preferences in over 19,000 people aged 18 to 68 and found that everyone at every age reported enormous change behind them and predicted almost none ahead — the end-of-history illusion. Change per decade does fall with age. But r(t) = e^−(t−13)/τ is my invention, a shape picked because it reproduces your two intuitions. It is a metaphor engine with one dial showing, not a measurement.
And your core claim already has support you didn't know about In a 16-to-35 sample, psychosocial maturity predicted risk-taking behaviour better than chronological age did. That's your whole thesis in one result: the number on the birth certificate is a worse instrument than the thing it's standing in for. Steinberg's dual-systems work says the same — cognitive capacity arrives around 16, psychosocial maturity keeps arriving into the mid-20s, and the two come apart, which is exactly the gap your "16 acting 45" and your "30 acting 13" are both living in.
One thing the model can't see Developmental distance is not the same as leverage. Two people can sit half a unit apart on this ruler and still be wildly asymmetric in money, housing, visa status, language, social network, or who can walk away cheaply. That asymmetry is what "manipulative" usually names, and it doesn't live on this axis at all. v1.2, if you want it: same warp, but the y-axis is exit cost.
