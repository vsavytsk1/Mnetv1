How the rigs work (same grains, different container) +

Every rig runs the identical grain engine — Newtonian discrete elements on flat typed arrays, with a contact graph rebuilt each step. The only thing that changes between rigs is the boundary: a jar, a funnel, or a basket with a piston. One physics, three containers.

The Pile. Grains are nodes, contacts are edges. Gold edges are force chains — the branching skeleton that carries the load while neighbours coast. When the average contacts-per-grain (the coordination number) passes ~4, the network goes rigid and jams. Shake intensity Γ = A(2πf)²/g compares your shake to gravity; past ~1.3 the network melts and grains flow.

The Hopper. Narrow the outlet toward a couple of grain diameters and the grains bridge it: a self-supporting arch of force chains spans the hole and flow stops dead, even with a clear gap and gravity pulling. Real silo jamming — the bane of every coffee hopper. A tap (the same Γ shake) breaks the arch and flow resumes.

The Tamp. A piston presses the puck. Watch the force chains light up, the coordination number climb as grains gain neighbours, and the puck lose height as it densifies. The walls carry part of the load through friction (the Janssen effect), so the bottom feels less than the top.

Honest scale: a real 300 g jar holds millions of ~300 µm grains. Each rig shows a couple hundred coarse stand-ins in one 2D slice. The grain count is scaled down; the behaviour — force chains, jamming, arching, compaction — is the real, emergent thing, not scripted.
