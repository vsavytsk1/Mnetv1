RIGID AND MOBILE ARE ONE MATRIX

m = 2V − 3 − rank(R)   (degrees of freedom)
s = E − rank(R)   (redundant bars / self-stress)
m − s = 2V − 3 − E   (Maxwell · Calladine · always)
Strip a machine to its skeleton: rigid bars joined at pin joints. Build the rigidity matrix R — one row per bar, holding the direction it can push or pull. The kernel of R is every motion that keeps all bars the right length; three of those are just sliding and spinning the whole thing (the rigid-body motions). Whatever kernel is left over is a real degree of freedom — the structure flexing. Count it and you get m.

m = 0 → the frame is rigid. It drives, it bears load, it does not move. m ≥ 1 → it is a mechanism. It transforms, it moves — and it cannot hold a load. Here is the whole wall: a load-bearing state needs m=0, a transformation needs m≥1, and no single fixed edge set is both. To transform you must remove a bar — and m snaps from 0 to ≥1.

So a real Transformer cannot be one rigid object that folds. It must change which graph it is mid-motion (a metamorphic mechanism), unlock and relock its own joints, and pay an energy barrier to cross — a bill the square-cube law inflates without mercy: actuation demand climbs as L⁴ while the muscle to pay it climbs only as L³. A toy crosses for almost nothing. A five-tonne car cannot. The movie's cube makes the crossing free. Mother Nature sends the invoice — every single time.
K1 · R, rank, m, s and the amber redundant-bar highlight are real, recomputed live from the graph and the current joint coordinates. The animated flex is a true non-trivial kernel vector of R — the actual DOF moving.
K2 · 2D and generic. Laman (E=2V−3 and every subgraph ≤ 2V′−3) makes 2D rigidity purely combinatorial. In 3D there is no known clean combinatorial law — the "double banana" passes the count yet flexes. That gap is an open problem, not laziness.
K3 · rank is computed at the current coordinates. Drag joints collinear and rank can drop — extra "paradoxical" motion appears. Not a bug: that is exactly how Bennett/Bricard overconstrained linkages move, on special geometry.
